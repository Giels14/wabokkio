# Sun safety with toddlers on holiday: shade, hats and smart sunscreen

Sun belongs on holiday. Burnt little shoulders don’t. With toddlers, sun safety works best as a layer system — not as one bottle of SPF that has to solve everything.

## Shade is layer one

Seek trees, umbrellas, beach tents or the stroller canopy. Babies under six months should stay out of direct sun as much as possible. Toddlers too: plan outdoor play outside the harshest hours, roughly 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., or swap in shade breaks.

## Clothes and hat: layer two

Light, tightly woven clothes or long-sleeve UV swimwear cover more than cream alone. A wide-brim hat (ideally with a chin strap) protects face, ears and neck. Kids’ sunglasses with solid UV protection are a bonus if your child will keep them on.

## Apply smart: layer three

Choose broad-spectrum sunscreen (UVA and UVB) with at least SPF 30. Apply 15 to 30 minutes before you go out, generously — especially face, ears, hands, feet and the backs of knees. Reapply about every two hours, and sooner after swimming, sweating or towelling off. Cloudy weather still counts: a large share of UV gets through, and sand and water bounce rays back.

## Babies and sensitive skin

For the youngest, shade and covering clothes matter most; sunscreen on small exposed patches can sometimes help, but check with your doctor if unsure. If irritation appears, try a mineral product with zinc oxide or titanium dioxide.

## Drink and cool down

Offer water often, even if your child doesn’t ask. Redness, crankiness or overheating? Straight into shade, lighter clothes, cool with lukewarm water. Blisters, fever, or a baby under one with a burn: contact a doctor.

## Make it a ritual

“Hat on, cream on, then play” as a fixed little song prevents arguments. You do the same. Example works faster than explanation.


## Don’t forget reflection and altitude

On the beach, by the pool or on snow, UV bounces back; you can burn faster than you think, even near the edge of shade. At higher altitudes the sun is stronger. Pack enough cream (minis for the day bag, a bigger tube for the room) and set a phone reminder to reapply — because between buckets and waves you forget the clock.
