# Beach holiday with a toddler: sand, sea and keeping your cool

A toddler on the beach is magic: first ripples, buckets, wet trousers from pure joy. It’s also sand in the sandwich and “where did that one sandal go?”. A few steady routines tip the day toward fun.

## Shade first, then play

Set up the umbrella, beach tent or shade cloth first. Swimwear, hat and play come after. For babies under six months, shade is the main protection. For toddlers, the shady base stays the place to eat, nap and cool down. Pick a spot near the toilets or an exit, because toddlers announce a nappy emergency with ten seconds' warning.

## Go early or late

Harsh midday hours are tough on little bodies. Early morning or late afternoon means softer light, cooler sand and often a quieter beach. Spend midday napping back at the room. You haven't wasted a holiday day; you've planned it well.

## Gear that works on sand

A regular stroller sinks. Take a carrier for the walk across the sand, or a beach wagon for bags and child. Water shoes protect feet against hot stones. Swim nappies leak, so pack a regular nappy for the ride back. A mesh bag or wet bag lets you shake the sand out on the spot.

## Food and drink along

Toddlers forget to drink when they’re digging. Bring water and offer it often. A small cooler with fruit, cheese and familiar snacks prevents hangry moments far from the snack bar. Eat a solid meal before you head to the beach; light snacks there keep you going.

## Boundaries in the sand

Mark a “home base” with towels or bags. Stay within arm’s reach of the water: ripples are fun but unpredictable. For older toddlers who already dare to go in, a well-fitting life jacket helps more than floaties alone.

## Pack the night before

Lay out swimwear, fill bottles, check sunscreen: in the morning you only grab the bag. Less chaos, more playtime.
